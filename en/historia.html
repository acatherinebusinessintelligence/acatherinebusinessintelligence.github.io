My story

Where does DraCorazónIA come from?

When my heart and my brain taught me to give technology a new purpose.

1 When everything stopped

For many years I tried to fulfil every role in my life: mother, wife, professional and independent woman. I wanted to work, support myself, study and keep growing, without letting down the people and responsibilities that mattered most to me.

As a systems engineer, I had spent years working with technology, data and business solutions. I wanted to give all that knowledge a name and academic backing. I was interested in studying Supply Chain Management, but just before the pandemic the chance came to do a master's in Big Data and Analytics, a field that matched my experience and what I truly enjoyed.

But between work, studies, family and the demands I placed on myself, I began to feel overwhelmed. I tried to keep up with everything, without realizing I was reaching my own limits.

Until my heart and my brain began to fail.

One of the hardest moments came while I was giving a lecture to a group of students at a company. Suddenly, my mind went blank. I could not recognize where I was or the people in front of me.

Then came the medical tests, the questions and the uncertainty. An electroencephalogram found abnormal theta activity in the left frontal and temporal regions. I especially remember the neurologist's words: I had to reduce my overload.

But something troubled me deeply: how could I stop when I still had so many dreams, projects and knowledge to share?

2 Learning again

Studying had always been my passion, and also a way to escape some realities. So I kept studying. After telematics, Big Data and business analytics, it was time for artificial intelligence. I wanted it to complement my work: I had already done some things since my undergraduate studies, and I knew that going deeper would take me far.

But fear took over. I was investing my savings and sacrificing time.

At first it wasn't easy. I struggled to concentrate, I got frustrated when something didn't work, and I was afraid of making mistakes, of my mind going blank again. There were days when I thought I could no longer learn the way I used to.

So I stopped demanding everything of myself at once. I started with small goals: one topic, one exercise, a few lines of code. I made mistakes, looked at what had gone wrong, corrected it and tried again. When something overwhelmed me, I broke it into smaller pieces. Little by little, what had seemed impossible began to feel possible.

3 A study companion

I knew there were people with more training, more ease, or who simply knew more than I did. For a while that weighed on me. But in artificial intelligence I found a study companion: someone I could ask again and again without fear of seeming slow; who explained a concept in different ways until I understood it, and who helped me review my mistakes instead of hiding them. And studying how machines learn helped me understand how the brain learns.

I didn't ask it to think for me. I asked it to help me think better.

4 From learning to creating

Artificial intelligence didn't just help me study: it also let me create. What once would have taken a development team, I could now build myself, step by step. That is how web labs, simulators and tutors for my classes were born, along with tools that helped me organize and preserve my knowledge, automate tasks and reduce the load I had carried for years.

Without realizing it, I designed those tools the way AI had helped me: they don't give students the answer; they guide them to think, make mistakes, correct and move forward in stages.

It was not about replacing my abilities or finding a cure through technology. It was about learning to work differently, respecting my limits and continuing to do what I loved: teaching, researching, building and sharing knowledge.

5 What science helped me understand

Over time I understood why this path worked.

That blank mind had an explanation: intense stress weakens the prefrontal cortex, the region that sustains attention and working memory. Reducing my overload wasn't giving up; it was giving my brain the conditions to work.

Mistakes weren't a sign of incapacity either. As neuroscientist Andrew Huberman explains, errors signal to the nervous system that it needs to adjust. Research on learning shows that making mistakes and correcting them with feedback helps us learn, and that treating ourselves kindly after a failure motivates us to improve more than harsh self-criticism does.

Moving forward step by step wasn't a coincidence. In a study with adult barn owls, the brain adapted when changes came gradually, but not when they came all at once. It is a study in birds and doesn't prove the same in people, but it described very well what I was living.

And AI as a tutor made sense: we have known for decades that personal tutoring transforms learning, and recent studies show that AI can bring it to more people, especially those who start at a disadvantage, as long as it is used to understand and not as a shortcut.

Santiago Ramón y Cajal had written it more than a century ago:

“Every man can, if he so desires, become the sculptor of his own brain.”

Finding learning hard didn't mean I couldn't do it. It wasn't about seeking fear or suffering, but about giving myself permission to be a beginner.

6 The birth

And that is where DraCorazónIA began to take shape.

Dra.

For my calling to research, study and seek answers through scientific knowledge.

Corazón

Heart: because it was part of my story, and because it stands for life, family, empathy and the humanity no technology should make us forget.

IA

AI: because I found in artificial intelligence an ally to build solutions, expand our capabilities and support people.

What began as a personal search became a purpose that now goes beyond my own experience.

DraCorazónIA represents my commitment to applied, ethical and responsible artificial intelligence: technology that not only transforms processes but also cares for people, preserves knowledge and helps build a more humane society.

Because one day I understood that I didn't need to stop doing what I loved. I needed to find a different way to keep going.

And that is the essence of DraCorazónIA.

Artificial intelligence with knowledge, purpose and heart.
